# Beginner CW contact research

## Common shape of a first contact

A simple CW QSO begins with one station calling CQ and giving its call sign. Another station replies with both call signs. The operators then exchange an RST signal report, names, and locations (QTH), and close with 73. A first lesson can omit equipment details and longer small talk.

Two examples informed the planned flow:

- [QRP Labs — Beginner's CW QSO](https://qrp-labs.com/qcx/cwqso.html) gives a worked contact that starts with CQ, repeats call signs, exchanges RST/name/QTH, and closes with 73. It explains K as an invitation to reply and KN as a reply restricted to the addressed station.
- [KA1DDB — QSO Template](https://www.qsl.net/ka1ddb/qsotemplate.html) provides separate templates for calling CQ and answering CQ. Its basic exchange covers call signs, RST, name, and QTH before signing off.
- [ARRL — Making Your First Contact](https://www.arrl.org/making-your-first-contact) describes CQ as a general call and recommends beginning with basic contact information. This article discusses voice operating too, so the CW examples above guide the Morse-specific wording.

## Terms used by the first lesson

| Term | Plain-language meaning |
| --- | --- |
| CQ | General call to any station |
| DE | From; separates the called and calling station |
| K | Invitation to transmit |
| UR | Your |
| RST | Readability, signal strength, and tone report |
| QTH | Location |
| TNX / FER | Thanks / for |
| FB | Fine business; an affirmative or friendly acknowledgment |
| QSO | A two-way contact |
| 73 | Best regards |
| SK | End of contact |

## Repeat and speed requests for later lessons

“PSE AGN” asks the other operator to send again. “QRS” asks them to send more slowly. A future repeat feature should replay the last bot transmission and, for QRS, lower its sending speed. This is a learning interaction in the simulation; the first release keeps the bot's scripted turns fixed.

## Script choice

Use a short fictional exchange inspired by the examples, rather than copying an operator's full transcript. Keep the exact target text visible and avoid optional words in the first lesson. A later free-form lesson can accept natural variations.
